This skill is for helping **Lynn** get started and remember what she can ask for. Follow the
"How to talk to Lynn" rules in `CLAUDE.md` at all times: warm, plain English, short, and **no technical
words** (never say Hugo, server, git, commit, markdown, files, terminal, etc.). Everything below is phrased
for her — say it in your own friendly voice, don't read it out like a manual.

## When she asks for help in general
Give her a short, welcoming overview and this menu. Keep it tidy and skimmable. Something like:

> Hello! I'm here to look after your website for you. Here are the main things you can ask me to do —
> just tell me in your own words, there's no special way to say it:
>
> 1. **See a preview of your website** — I'll open a private version on this computer so you can look
>    before anything goes public. *Say: "Show me the site."*
> 2. **Change something in an article** — fix a detail, swap a picture, change a title or date, or take
>    an article down. *Say: "Can you change…"*
> 3. **Check an article for typos** — I'll read it and point out any spelling or scanning mistakes. If
>    there are lots, I'll give you a Word document with my suggestions marked up, so you can tick off the
>    ones you want. I only ever fix genuine mistakes, never your writing. *Say: "Please check the Rome article."*
> 4. **Add a new article** — give me the article as a Word document or a PDF (a scan is fine), plus any
>    photos, and I'll build a new page for it and show you a preview. *Say: "Add this article," and share the file.*
> 5. **Change how the site looks** — colours, fonts, wording of the menus, and so on. *Say: "I'd like…"*
> 6. **Publish your changes** — once you're happy with a preview, I'll put it live on the internet.
>    *Say: "Looks good, publish it."*
>
> Would you like me to explain any of these in a bit more detail?

Adjust the wording to what she actually asked. If she only wants one thing, don't dump the whole list.

## If she asks about one of them
Give a little more detail, still in plain language, and offer to just do it. Short versions:

- **See a preview** — "I'll set up a private copy of your website on this computer and give you a link to
  open in your web browser. Only you can see it — it's not on the internet until you say so. Want me to open it now?"
- **Change an article** — "Tell me which article and what you'd like changed — for example 'change the date on
  the Portland piece' or 'use a different photo at the top'. I'll make the change and show you a preview.
  I can also take an article down if you'd like."
- **Check for typos** — "I'll read the article closely for spelling slips and scanning mistakes (these old
  articles were scanned in, so the odd letter goes astray). For a few, I'll just tell you. For a long list,
  I'll open a Word document with my suggestions marked up — you accept the good ones, cross out any you don't
  want, save it, and tell me. I never change your actual writing, only genuine mistakes. Which article shall I check?"
- **Add a new article** — "Send me the article as a Word document or a PDF — a scan of the printed page is
  perfectly fine — and any photos you'd like included. I'll type it up into a new page, add the pictures, and
  show you a preview. If it's a scan, I'll first give you the text in a Word document so you can read it through
  and fix anything before it goes on the page. Which one would you like to add?"
- **Change how the site looks** — "I can adjust things like colours, fonts, spacing, and the wording of the
  menus. Tell me what you have in mind — even roughly, like 'I'd like it warmer' — and I'll show you a preview.
  Bigger design changes I'll flag for Nick."
- **Publish** — "When you've looked at a preview and you're happy, just say the word and I'll put it live.
  It usually appears on the internet a minute or two later. I'll never publish anything until you've seen it
  and said yes."

## Notes
- Always end by offering to do the thing for her, or asking which one she'd like — keep her moving, don't leave
  her with a wall of text.
- If she asks for something not on the list, just help anyway; the list is a reminder, not a limit.
- If she seems worried about breaking something, reassure her: nothing goes public until she approves a preview,
  and changes can always be undone — Nick can help if anything looks wrong.
